Exact magnetic geodesics on the flat torus

About this simulation

Mathematical setup

The simulation lives on the flat torus $\mathbb{T}^2 = \mathbb{R}^2/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^2$, parametrised by coordinates $(r,\theta)\in[0,2\pi)^2$. The Riemannian metric is the flat product metric $$ds^2 = dr^2 + d\theta^2,$$ i.e.\ the profile function $\varphi(r)\equiv 1$. The torus has constant zero sectional curvature; each coordinate circle has circumference $2\pi$. The parameter square is the fundamental domain with opposite edges identified: $r\sim r+2\pi$ and $\theta\sim\theta+2\pi$.

Fundamental group and winding classes

The fundamental group of the torus is $\pi_1(\mathbb{T}^2)=\mathbb{Z}^2$, generated by the two non-contractible loops $\gamma_r: t\mapsto(2\pi t,0)$ and $\gamma_\theta: t\mapsto(0,2\pi t)$. Two points $A=(r_0,\theta_0)$ and $B=(r_1,\theta_1)$ can be joined by paths in infinitely many homotopy classes, each labelled by a winding class $(k,l)\in\mathbb{Z}^2$: the path winds $k$ full turns in the $r$-direction and $l$ full turns in the $\theta$-direction. In the universal cover $\mathbb{R}^2$, the class-$(k,l)$ path connects $(r_0,\theta_0)$ to the lifted point $(r_1+2\pi k,\;\theta_1+2\pi l)$. The energy-minimising representative in each class is the straight segment of length $$L_{k,l} = \sqrt{(\Delta r + 2\pi k)^2 + (\Delta\theta + 2\pi l)^2}, \quad \Delta r := r_1-r_0,\;\Delta\theta := \theta_1-\theta_0.$$ The simulation considers all $(k,l)\in\{-1,0,1\}^2$, giving nine candidate geodesics; the globally shortest one is shown in gray.

Closed 1-forms and the magnetic cost

Every closed 1-form on $\mathbb{T}^2$ can be written $\eta = a\,dr + b\,d\theta$ for constants $a,b\in\mathbb{R}$. The forms $dr$ and $d\theta$ generate the de Rham cohomology $H^1(\mathbb{T}^2;\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}^2$; they are closed but not exact — their integrals around the generating loops equal $2\pi$ — so $\eta$ carries genuine topological information whenever $(a,b)\neq(0,0)$. The parameters $(a,b)$ are chosen via the XY pad.

The magnetic cost of a path $\gamma$ from $A$ to $B$ is $$C(\gamma) = L(\gamma) - \int_\gamma \eta.$$ Because $\eta$ is closed, its line integral depends only on the homotopy class: $$\int_{\gamma_{k,l}} \eta = a\,(\Delta r+2\pi k) + b\,(\Delta\theta+2\pi l).$$ Thus the magnetic cost in class $(k,l)$ is the explicit quantity $$C_{k,l}(a,b) = L_{k,l} - a(\Delta r+2\pi k) - b(\Delta\theta+2\pi l),$$ and the magnetic minimiser is the class $(k^*,l^*)$ achieving $\min_{k,l}C_{k,l}(a,b)$. The colored curve is the geodesic in this winding class; its color encodes the class. When $(a,b)=(0,0)$ the magnetic and Riemannian minimisers coincide.

Operator norm and the unit circle

The pointwise cotangent norm of $\eta=a\,dr+b\,d\theta$ at $x\in\mathbb{T}^2$ is $$\|\eta(x)\|^2_{T^*_x} = g^{rr}(x)\,a^2 + g^{\theta\theta}(x)\,b^2 = a^2 + \frac{b^2}{\varphi(r)^2}.$$ On the flat torus $\varphi\equiv1$, so $\|\eta\|_{op}=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}$ is constant across the torus. The unit ball $\|\eta\|_{op}=1$ is the circle $a^2+b^2=1$, drawn as the purple ellipse (a circle in this case) on the XY pad. For $\|\eta\|_{op}<1$, one has $C_{k,l}\geq L_{k,l}(1-\|\eta\|_{op})>0$ for all classes, so the magnetic cost is always positive and the variational problem is well-posed. The dot on the XY pad turns red when approaching the unit circle as a visual warning.

The magnetic Brunn–Minkowski inequality

This simulation is a visual tool for studying the magnetic Brunn–Minkowski inequality. Given measurable sets $A,B\subset\mathbb{T}^2$ and a closed 1-form $\eta$ with $\|\eta\|_{op}<1$, define the magnetic midpoint set $$M_\eta(A,B) = \bigl\{\,\gamma_{1/2}^{(a,b)}: a\in A,\; b\in B\,\bigr\},$$ where $\gamma^{(a,b)}$ is the magnetic minimiser from $a$ to $b$ and $\gamma_{1/2}^{(a,b)}$ is its midpoint (the unique point at half the arc length along $\gamma$). The classical Brunn–Minkowski inequality on $\mathbb{R}^n$ asserts $|M(A,B)|^{1/n}\geq(|A|^{1/n}+|B|^{1/n})/2$; the magnetic analogue asks whether a lower bound of similar shape holds for $|M_\eta(A,B)|$, with a constant $c(\eta)$ depending on $\eta$ and the geometry.

The XY pad phase diagram reveals a key feature: as $(a,b)$ varies, the magnetic minimiser can jump discontinuously to a different winding class. These phase boundaries are where two winding classes have equal cost: $$C_{k,l}(a,b) = C_{k',l'}(a,b) \;\Longleftrightarrow\; a(2\pi(k-k')) + b(2\pi(l-l')) = L_{k,l} - L_{k',l'},$$ which is a family of linear equations in $(a,b)$ — straight lines in the XY pad. The resulting patchwork of colored regions directly shows which winding class wins at each $\eta$. On the flat torus all phase boundaries are straight, reflecting the linear dependence of $C_{k,l}$ on $(a,b)$.

Cloud mode and the midpoint map

In cloud mode the single points $A$, $B$ are replaced by small clouds of $N_c=19$ sample points distributed over discs of radius $0.25$ around each point (using a sunflower/Fibonacci lattice; see below). For all $N_c^2=361$ cross-pairs $(a_i,b_j)\in\mathrm{cloudA}\times\mathrm{cloudB}$, the magnetic minimiser and its midpoint are computed. The gray dots show the Riemannian midpoints (midpoints of the shortest Riemannian geodesics) and the colored dots show the magnetic midpoints, colored by winding class. Together they approximate $M_\eta(A,B)$, providing a visual estimate of its area and shape as $\eta$ varies on the XY pad. Observe how the midpoint cloud can split into multiple clusters when $A\times B$ straddles a phase boundary.

The vector field η♯

Under the metric $ds^2=dr^2+d\theta^2$, the musical sharp isomorphism $\flat^{-1}: T^*\mathbb{T}^2\to T\mathbb{T}^2$ sends $\eta=a\,dr+b\,d\theta$ to $\eta^\sharp = a\,\partial_r + b\,\partial_\theta$. On the flat torus $\eta^\sharp$ is a constant vector field: all arrows in the 2D plot are parallel and of equal length, pointing in the direction $(b,a)$ in $(\theta,r)$-canvas coordinates. Arrow length and opacity scale with $\|\eta\|$, vanishing at $\eta=0$. (On the torus of revolution the arrows vary with $r$ because $\varphi(r)$ is non-constant; here they are uniform.)

Technical: geodesic computation

On $\mathbb{T}^2$ with $ds^2=dr^2+d\theta^2$ the geodesic equations are $\ddot r=\ddot\theta=0$, so geodesics are straight lines in $\mathbb{R}^2$. For each $(k,l)\in\{-1,0,1\}^2$, the code computes the lifted displacement $\mathbf{d}_{k,l}=(\Delta r+2\pi k,\,\Delta\theta+2\pi l)$ and sets $L_{k,l}=|\mathbf{d}_{k,l}|$. The path is sampled at $N+1=61$ equally spaced points $(r_0+t_i d_r,\,\theta_0+t_i d_\theta)$, $t_i=i/N$, $i=0,\ldots,N$. No numerical integration is required. The geodesic midpoint is the average of the lifted endpoints reduced modulo $2\pi$. Total operation count per pair: nine square-roots and nine subtractions.

The magnetic minimiser is found by evaluating $C_{k,l}=L_{k,l}-a\,d_r-b\,d_\theta$ for all nine classes and taking the minimum — nine multiply-adds. For the cloud of $361$ pairs the entire computation (geodesics + minimisers) finishes in under 5 ms on a modern browser, running synchronously on the main thread.

Technical: phase diagram

The XY pad shows a $21\times21$ phase diagram precomputed for the current positions of $A$ and $B$. At each grid point $(a_i,b_j)\in[-0.65,0.65]^2$ with step $0.065$, the code evaluates all nine costs $\{C_{k,l}(a_i,b_j)\}$ and records the minimising class as a string key (e.g.\ "-1,0"). Each of the nine classes is assigned a fixed RGB colour from the classRGB table. Grid points with $a_i^2+b_j^2\geq0.98$ are masked to stay within the operator-norm unit ball. The phase cells are drawn as filled rectangles on the XY canvas; the crosshair and dot show the current $(a,b)$. The diagram is recomputed from scratch on every A/B drag event. Because the phase boundaries are straight lines on the flat torus, the diagram has a clean linear-Voronoi appearance.

Technical: Fibonacci lattice cloud

The 19-point cloud around a centre $p=(r_0,\theta_0)$ is generated by the sunflower (Fibonacci) lattice: $$p_k = \bigl(r_0 + \rho_k\cos(k\psi),\;\theta_0+\rho_k\sin(k\psi)\bigr)\!\!\pmod{2\pi}, \quad k=0,\ldots,18,$$ where $\psi=\pi(3-\sqrt{5})\approx2.3998\,\mathrm{rad}$ is the golden angle and $\rho_k=0.25\sqrt{k/18}$ (with $\rho_0=0$ placing one point at the exact centre). The radial spacing $\rho_k\propto\sqrt{k}$ distributes points uniformly in area over the disc of radius $0.25$, and the golden-angle rotation ensures good angular separation, avoiding clustering artefacts common in equal-angle spirals or random placement. All coordinates are reduced modulo $2\pi$ to stay in the fundamental domain.

Technical: η♯ arrow field

At each point of a $9\times11$ grid in $(r,\theta)$ parameter space the code evaluates $\eta^\sharp=(a,\,b)$ (in canvas coordinates: $u_x=b$, $u_y=-a$), normalises to unit Euclidean length, and draws a centred arrow of half-length $\propto\|\eta\|/\|\eta\|_{\max}$ with a small arrowhead (half-angle $0.45$ rad, tip at $+0.5$ shaft). On the flat torus $\varphi\equiv1$ so all arrows are identical; the variation across $r$-rows seen on the torus of revolution is absent here.

Technical: rendering

2D parameter space uses the Canvas 2D API. Geodesic paths are stored as lifted (unwrapped) coordinate arrays. The renderer applies $\bmod\,2\pi$ to each point when computing canvas pixel positions, and detects genuine periodic-boundary crossings by checking whether adjacent modded values differ by more than $\pi$; such jumps issue a moveTo rather than a lineTo, preventing spurious wrap-around lines. The XY pad is a separate $110\times110$ canvas redrawn on every interaction event. All computation runs synchronously on the main thread — flat-torus geodesic computation is $O(1)$ per pair and the full cloud update takes under 5 ms.
